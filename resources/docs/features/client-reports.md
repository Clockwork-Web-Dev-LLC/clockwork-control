Client reports are the monthly "here's what we did for your site" email. Everything lives in the `client_reports` module (`modules/ClientReports/`), and the sidebar entry is **Client reports** (`/client-reports`). Turn the module on or off from the Module Directory like any other module. When it's off, the routes and nav item go away.

Open issues, decisions and next steps are tracked separately in [Internal → Client reports tracker](/docs/internal/client-reports-tracker).

## The tabs

The module has four tabs: **Clients** (the landing page), **Reports**, **Scheduling** and **Templates**. The Reports tab shows how many of the latest period's reports are waiting for someone, and the Scheduling tab how many care-plan sites have no report schedule.

**Clients** (`/client-reports`, route `client-reports.index`) lists every client (the Client Management module's clients; sites belong to a client through `sites.client_id`) with, per site: the care plan (**Care plan**, or **No care plan — reports won't send**), the schedule (**Scheduled · auto/draft**, **Schedule disabled**, or **No schedule** with a one-click **Create schedule** for a care-plan site), its recipients, and a strip of the last six report periods. Each month's cell links to that month's report and is coloured by its state: sent, needs review, outdated, ready to send, partly sent, **skipped** (the schedule existed but the site had no care plan) or no report. The list is **organised by care plan**. A client counts as a care-plan client when any of its active sites has a care plan (its other sites stay listed with it, marked **No care plan — reports won't send**). By default the page lists the care-plan clients under **Care plan clients — reported monthly**, and folds everyone else into a collapsed **No care plan — reports aren't sent (N)** section below. The **Care plan / No care plan / All** switch changes that: **No care plan** lists only those, **All** lists both, care-plan clients first. Sites without a client follow the same split: an **Unassigned sites** entry after the care-plan clients holds the active care-plan ones, and another in the No care plan section holds the rest. **Order: Needs attention** (the default) puts clients with a report waiting or a care-plan site missing a schedule first, then A–Z; **A–Z** is alphabetical (the Unassigned entry after the clients either way). Archived sites are left out; inactive ones are dimmed and never count as missing a schedule. Filters: search (client, contact, site or recipient), **Reports waiting** (a site's newest report is waiting for review or sending) and **Missing a schedule**, applied to both sections. The switch, the order and the filters all live in the query string, so paging and the client pages keep them, and the four tiles count the current care-plan selection (the waiting and no-schedule tiles are those filters).

Click a client for its **reports page** (`/client-reports/clients/{id}`, or `/client-reports/clients/unassigned`): the client's contacts and care-plan summary, then each site with its schedule (status, mode, recipients, next run, last sent, **Edit**, **Enable/Disable**) and the full history of its reports, each with its state, short hold-reason chips and **View**, **Email**, **Recompile**, **Send** and **Test to me**. Replaced copies are hidden behind a "Show replaced copies" link. **Previous**/**Next** (or ← / →) step through the client list in the order it was shown, with the care-plan switch, order and filters it was opened with (the folded No care plan clients come after the care-plan ones), and **Go to client…** jumps to any client by name.

**Reports** (`/client-reports/reports`, route `client-reports.reports`) lists one period at a time, the latest by default, and hides replaced (superseded) copies unless **Show replaced copies** is ticked. Filter by period, status (**Waiting**, i.e. anything unsent, **Needs review**, **Outdated**, **Ready to send**, **Partly sent**, **Sent**, **Replaced**), hold reason and a search over site, client and recipient. The summary tiles count the period's reports per status and are filter links too. Every filter lives in the query string, so paging, opening a report and coming back keep it. Each row shows short chips for why a report is held (hover for the full sentences) and labelled actions.

Hold-reason categories are worked out from the stored `meta.review_reasons` sentences by `Modules\ClientReports\Support\ReviewReasonCategories` (keyword rules, most specific first; anything unrecognised is **Other**): First report, Malware & core files (including blocklists and core-file checks), Vulnerable plugins, Updates (including a silent Companion), Backups, Uptime, Traffic, Contact forms and Partial month. If `ReportReviewFlags` rewords a reason, check that `tests/Unit/Modules/ClientReportReviewCategoriesTest.php` still puts it in the right category. Report states come from `Modules\ClientReports\Support\ReportState`: **Ready to send** means nothing to fix, either a clean draft or a report held only because it's its schedule's first.

**Recompile all outdated (N)** recompiles every outdated, unsent scheduled report of the selected period; **Recompile selected** does the ticked ones. Both go through the same `ScheduledReportRunner::recompile()` as the Recompile button, at most 100 per press, never send, and say how many were recompiled, held, skipped or failed.

The one-off **Generate a one-off report** form at the bottom still compiles any site and date range by hand. Pick a site, a template, a date range, and optionally a client name, email and a custom note. You land on the preview. A period is always whole days: the compiler runs from the start of the first day to the end of the last, and `meta.period_days` is a whole number (31 for October).

**The operator preview** (`/client-reports/{report}`) has a sticky toolbar, on the operator preview only: **Back to list** (or **Back to client**) with the filters and page it was opened from, **Previous**/**Next** through that same filtered list, **Email version**, **Recompile**, **Print / PDF**, **Test to me** and **Send**. Send is disabled with the reason shown when the report is outdated, replaced or already with everyone. Keys: ← / → previous and next, **e** the email version. The review reasons are grouped by category, each with a link to the matching site dashboard tab (Security, Updates, Traffic, Forms or the overview), or to the schedule for a first report.

The **Email version** (`/client-reports/{report}/email`) shows the email exactly as the client gets it: From, To (who pressing Send would email now), Sent to, Subject, the grouped review reasons, and tabs for the HTML email and the plain-text version that text-only mail apps show, with the same previous/next and actions as the preview (← / → and **v** for the report page). The preview page lays sections out differently (updates as a table, for one), so check the email version before sending.

The preview, the HTML email and the plain-text email show the **same sections**: one rule, `Modules\ClientReports\Support\ReportSections::visibility()`, decides from the stored report which sections the client sees, and all three views use it. A section that was compiled but has nothing true to show (no hours logged, no form tests, backups not verified, the probe doesn't check the site, no speed test, traffic missing or not believable) is left out of all three. On the operator preview it shows instead as a grey, dashed note that doesn't print, saying why, e.g. "Work log hidden from the client: no hours were logged this period." The preview's updates table and its counts come from the same cleaned-up rows as the email (`ClientReportMail::updateRows()`: WordPress core, themes and plugins), and it has a Contact forms section with the email's wording.

The preview page is also what prints as the PDF and what the public `/reports/view/{token}` route serves. So operator-only things (the hidden-section notes and their badges, review reasons, the backup check or relay error, "no blocker reports to Control", the core-files "no checksum path" note, 429 blocked time, traffic coverage, the Recompile button and action messages, who logged each work-log entry) appear only on the operator preview, never print, and never appear on the public page.

An unsent scheduled report has a **Recompile** button (on the reports list, the client page and the operator preview). It compiles the same period again from current data and never sends anything; see [Refreshing held drafts](#refreshing-held-drafts-recompile).

**Send** emails a report to every address in its `client_email`, which scheduled reports fill with all their recipients, comma-separated. Before it sends, it asks for confirmation naming who gets it, and, for an `auto` schedule that has never sent, says that once this first report is sent the schedule emails its clean reports automatically. On a **Partly sent** report it only emails the addresses that don't have it yet; on a **Sent** one it sends to everyone again. If a report has no address saved, the button asks for one (you can enter several, comma-separated) and saves it on the report. A **superseded** report can't be sent. Sending a scheduled report by hand stamps its schedule's `last_sent_at`, the same as an automatic send.

**Test to me** (`POST /client-reports/{report}/send-test`) emails the exact client email to the signed-in operator's own address and nobody else, with `[TEST]` at the start of the subject (`ClientReportTestMail`). It works on any report and changes nothing: the report isn't marked sent, nobody is added to `sent_to`, and the schedule's `last_sent_at` (which switches a schedule to sending by itself) stays as it was.

States: **Needs review** (held, see below), **Outdated** (unsent and compiled under older report rules; it can't be sent until it's recompiled), **Ready to send** (nothing to fix: a clean draft, or held only as a schedule's first report), **Partly sent** (some recipients got it), **Sent**, and **Replaced** (`superseded`: a newer report for the same schedule and period replaced it before it reached anyone, after a recompile or a scheduled rerun).

**Templates** (`/client-reports/templates`) control which sections go into a report. A template is just a name plus a list of section keys. Exactly one template is the default. You can't delete the default or the last remaining template, and un-ticking "default" on the only default is silently ignored.

**Scheduling** (`/client-reports/schedules`) allows one schedule per site, enforced by a unique index on `site_id`. Each schedule has a frequency (`weekly` / `monthly`), a recipients list, a template, and a delivery mode:

- **`auto`** compiles and emails every recipient, unless something needs a person first, in which case it's held as a draft (see **Hold for review**). A schedule's **first** report is always held. It needs at least one recipient.
- **`draft`** only compiles, so someone can review the report before sending it by hand. Drafts are flagged with the same review reasons.

Schedules only send for **care-plan sites** (see below); the list marks the others **No care plan — won't send**. The list shows care-plan schedules first (**Care plan — reported monthly**) and folds the **No care plan — won't send** ones into a collapsed section below, which opens when you filter for them. It filters by why a schedule won't send (**No care plan**, **No recipients**, **Disabled**) or by **Drafts for review**, with counts, searches by site, client or recipient, and shows each schedule's latest report and its state.

**Care-plan sites without a report schedule** (active, not archived) are listed at the top: that's the coverage gap to close. **Create schedule** sets one up in one click (`POST /client-reports/schedules/quick/{site}`): the default template, monthly, next run on the 1st, `auto` to the client's contacts (the same fallback recipients WordPress is shown: the client's emails, else the site's `client_email`), or `draft` with no recipients when there are none. **Create all N** does it for every one of them. Nothing is sent: an `auto` schedule's first report is always held for review.

Each row also has **Pause/Enable** and **Send now** buttons. **Send now** runs the schedule for **last month**, exactly like the 06:00 run, so on a schedule that has sent before it emails a clean report straight away. To refresh a held report, use **Recompile** on the report instead.

## A report is a snapshot

This is the key thing to understand. `ClientReportCompiler::compile()` queries the monitoring tables once and writes everything into `client_reports.sections_data` as JSON. That includes the branding (colours, logo, company name), captured at generation time. The preview page and the email both render from that JSON and never from live data.

So if you change the branding or fix a data bug, existing reports won't change. Recompile an unsent scheduled report (see [Refreshing held drafts](#refreshing-held-drafts-recompile)), or generate a new one.

## Where each section's numbers come from

The rule for every section: **if we didn't check it during the period, the report says so** ("Not checked this month") or leaves it out. It never shows a passing default, because the email goes to clients as-is.

After deploying a schema bump, every unsent report shows **Outdated** until it's recompiled (see [Refreshing held drafts](#refreshing-held-drafts-recompile)). Keys added since (coverage start, 429 blocked time, traffic status, backup `suspect`/`failing`, `malware_truncated_checks`) are read with defaults, so older reports render as they always did. `meta.period_days` is the period's length in whole days (31 for October). Reports compiled before the honesty fixes have no version. The views treat their vulnerability line as "Not checked" and hide their forms numbers, because those were computed wrongly.

### Backup recording

`clockwork:record-site-backups` (core, `App\Services\Backups\SiteBackupRecorder`) reads every active site's backups from its source and upserts them into `site_backups`, keyed by source and the source's own id. It also writes one `site_backup_checks` row per site saying whether the read worked. Rows are **kept after the source prunes them**, so re-generating an old report still has the evidence. A failed read never deletes or zeroes anything; it only marks the check as failed, logs `site_backups.record_failed`, and makes the run exit as failed so it shows red in Scheduled Jobs. It's all reads: Pressable's backup endpoints are allowed in view-only mode.

Pressable's database backups are hourly, so one row per UTC day is kept, keyed `day:YYYY-MM-DD`; a later run's newer pick replaces it. A SpinupWP run is labelled by what's actually in the bucket (files, database, or both), and a run first seen mid-upload is replaced once the rest lands. Empty (0-byte) objects don't count as backups.

Section keys and labels are defined once in `ClientReportTemplate::SECTIONS`. Adding a section means adding it there, adding a `compileX()` method in the compiler, adding its show/hide rule (and hidden note) to `ReportSections`, and adding a block to the preview and both email parts. Wording shared by the three views (outage times, backup destination and kinds, sizes, data transferred, vulnerabilities, the malware label, the "no updates" sentence, the uptime colour, the coverage note and the operator's blocked-visitors note) lives in `Modules\ClientReports\Support\ReportPhrases`, so the report page says what both email parts say.

## Scheduled sending

**Every report waits for approval (on by default).** With approval on, the monthly run compiles and checks every report but **emails nothing**, whatever a schedule's delivery mode: each report lands as **Ready to send** (nothing to fix) or **Needs review** (held, with reasons). Operators send them from the **Reports** tab: one by one with **Send**, the ticked ones with **Send selected** (a ticked held report is sent too: ticking it is the approval), or every Ready to send report of the period with **Approve & send all ready (N)**. The bulk buttons send up to 25 reports per press through the same `ClientReportSender` as Send, skip outdated, replaced and already-sent reports, and stamp each schedule's `last_sent_at`; a press cut off part-way loses nothing, and pressing again only emails who is still owed it. The switch is on the **Scheduling** tab (**Let clean reports send themselves** / **Require approval**), stored as the `client_reports.require_approval` setting (`ScheduledReportRunner::approvalRequired()`), and each change is logged as `client_report_approval_changed`. With approval off, the rules below apply as written: Automatic schedules email clean reports by themselves.

`clockwork:send-client-reports` is registered by the module itself (`ClientReportsServiceProvider::scheduledTasks()`), not in `routes/console.php`. It runs daily at **06:00 app time** (UTC on our install). On each run:

1. It skips paused schedules, inactive sites, and schedules whose `next_run_at` is still in the future. It also skips sites **without a care plan**: reports are a care-plan service, and those sites get no nightly updates or security scans, so their report would be empty or wrong. The schedule still moves on to next month, so turning the care plan back on never sends a report for a month the site didn't have one. A site whose care plan was switched off only **after** the month ended (the Bill.com sync flips it at 01:30 UTC on the 1st, before this run) still gets that month compiled, held for a person to confirm (`meta.care_plan_ended_at`). The Bill.com sync logs each flip as a `care_plan_toggled` entry (`details.source = bill_com`), local only, never mirrored to the client's wp-admin. The Schedules list marks these **No care plan — won't send**, and **Send now** refuses them. Each run writes **one** `action_logs` entry (`client_report_skipped`, actor `scheduled`) listing the domains it skipped for having no care plan, with `details.reason = no_care_plan`, since a scheduled run's console output isn't kept.
2. It skips `auto` schedules that have no recipients.
3. It compiles **last calendar month** using the schedule's template, and saves the report with `schedule_id` set. For `auto`, if a report from this schedule already exists for the same period and isn't held, it **reuses that one** instead (see below), including one that's already been **sent** (for example finished by hand after a failed run), so nobody is emailed twice; a held one is recompiled.
4. **Hold for review.** Any report that hasn't reached anyone yet is checked by `ReportReviewFlags`. If anything needs a person first, an `auto` schedule keeps it as a draft instead of emailing it, the reasons are saved in `meta.review_reasons`, and the schedule moves on to next month. The history table shows **Needs review**, and the preview lists the reasons. Send it by hand from the history table once it's checked, or press **Recompile** first to refresh it (see [Refreshing held drafts](#refreshing-held-drafts-recompile)). Held reports are never sent automatically: running the schedule again (**Send now**, `--force`) in the same month recompiles last month from current data and checks again, and the older copy is marked **superseded** so it can't be sent by mistake. Draft-mode reports get the same check, so the first-run drafts show **Needs review** too, and any report that hasn't reached anyone is checked again before an auto send. The reasons show only in our preview, never on the public view. The rules (one reason each, worded for the reviewer):
   - **Coverage:** the care plan was switched off after the period ended (see step 1); the site was added after the period's first day ("Monitoring began Oct 19, so this report covers only part of the month."); separately, uptime monitoring began later than that ("Uptime monitoring began Oct 17, so uptime covers only part of the month."), or only after the period, when the Uptime section is left out. A care plan switched on part-way through the month holds it too ("The care plan was switched on Oct 28, so this report covers only part of the month."). Dates are read in the report timezone.
   - **Malware:** the latest scan came back `warning` or `issues_found` (the reason names the finding kinds and the scan id, e.g. "php_in_uploads ×4 (scan #123)", and says when the scan also stopped early); the latest clean scan was partial ("Partial scan": stopped at a limit, an SSH check hit its result cap, or an SSH scan from before cut-short scans were tracked); or an earlier scan in the period found malware that the latest one didn't; Sucuri SiteCheck reported malware or a blocklisting in the period on a site whose malware row comes from Companion (`sitecheck_last_issue_at`, `sitecheck_issues`); or no malware scan finished all month while the Companion or SiteCheck scans are switched on (`malware_expected`), so the client would read "Not checked this month". Review-tier findings a person marked reviewed no longer hold it (see **Mark findings reviewed** above).
   - **Core files and blocklists:** core files differ from the release; no core-file check ran although the host supports one; the site is on a blocklist; a core-file check or blocklist check earlier in the month found a problem that a later clean one hides (`checksum_last_issue_at`, `blacklist_last_issue_at`); no blocklist check finished all month while blocklist checks are on (`blacklist_expected`).
   - **Vulnerable plugins:** joined by the plugin's file slug, a plugin with a known vulnerability whose update waiting at the end of the month reaches the fixed version but wasn't applied, or whose updates are paused (auto-paused after failures, or by hand). A vulnerability on its own doesn't hold a report (about a third of sites have one at any time). The vulnerability list hasn't refreshed in 48 hours (`vulnerability_feed_stale`: every report would read "Not checked"), or an installed plugin has never had a successful lookup (`vulnerabilities_unchecked_plugins`, from the per-plugin lookup times `clockwork:refresh-plugin-vulnerabilities` keeps in the `plugin_vulnerabilities.last_lookups` setting), does hold it.
   - **Backups:** none found, stopped before the last 10 days, couldn't be checked, `suspect` (database backups far smaller than the database; for SpinupWP the run's own database half, `site_backups.database_bytes`, not the whole backup), every listed backup is files only (`no_database_backup`), the latest read of the source failed (`check_error`) or finished before the month ended (`read_before_period_end`), so the last days may be missing, `failing` (our relay has been failing, with its error), a daily source that missed more than 3 days of the days backups were expected (counted from when coverage began, `backups.expected_days`), or our relay's first archive coming 2 or more days after the period start (or after the site joined, if later) on a relay site: the S3 listing is the whole history, so its oldest archive is when archiving began, not where records start ("Off-site backups began on Oct 29, 2026; none were made before that this month …", kept as `archives_began`).
   - **Traffic:** `partial`, `suspect` or `no_data` (the section is left out and the reason says why).
   - **Contact forms:** lab checks not all accepted ("the forms accepted 18 of 19 automated check submissions"), or live tests where the site didn't report the email as sent.
   - **Uptime:** the probe hasn't checked the site lately; the site was still down at the end of the month; the monitor was blocked (HTTP 429) at the end of the month, which is said instead of "still down"; an outage of an hour or more during the month, with its date and length; the monitor was blocked (HTTP 429) for an hour or more in total (that time counts as up); the site was in maintenance mode for 2 hours or more in total (`maintenance_minutes`, also counted as up).
   - **Updates:** Companion hasn't reported in the 48h before the period end, so waiting updates and vulnerabilities are out of date; or no plugin, theme or core update was applied all month although some were known to be waiting (named in the snapshot, up to five, or applied after the period), so the client would read "No updates were applied this month" while they were failing or paused; or an update was undone after it ran (left out of the list, `updates.not_kept`).
   - **First report:** it's the schedule's first (`last_sent_at` is empty), so a new client's first email is always seen by a person. Send it by hand; after that, clean reports send themselves. This applies to `auto` schedules only, since a `draft` schedule's reports wait for a person anyway.
5. For `auto` schedules, it sends one email per recipient who isn't already in the report's `sent_to` (addresses compared ignoring case). Sends to one report run one at a time (a cache lock in `ClientReportSender`), so a double click or a hand send during the run can't email anyone twice. Once everyone has it, it marks the report `sent` and stamps `last_sent_at`.
6. It moves `next_run_at` forward: the 1st of next month for monthly schedules, or now plus 7 days at midnight for weekly ones. The midnight snap is on purpose, so the next 06:00 run sees it as due. Before this, runs used to slip a day every cycle. If any recipient failed, `next_run_at` **doesn't** move, so tomorrow's run retries.
7. If a site's report **crashed** (an exception while compiling or saving it), or any recipient's email **failed** (a mail outage, say), that site stays due and is retried tomorrow, the other sites carry on, and the command exits as **failed**, so the Scheduled Jobs row turns red. Crashes are in `laravel.log` as "Failed sending scheduled client report for …", failed recipients as "Client report #… failed to send to …". Scheduled Jobs only stores the status and duration, not the per-site lines; to see those, run the command by hand: sites already handled print "skipped (not due until …)", anything still due runs again.

The command and the **Send now** button share the same code: `ScheduledReportRunner`, with delivery in `ClientReportSender`. The **Recompile** action uses the same runner (`ScheduledReportRunner::recompile()`) but never reaches the sender.

**Recipient imports.** `clockwork:import-report-recipients <csv>` (dry run unless `--apply`) adds recipients to existing schedules and creates missing ones as monthly `auto` schedules. A CSV row is no proof the client pays for reports, so a schedule it creates for a site **without an active care plan** is created **disabled** (the dry run says "no care plan: created disabled"), unless `--include-no-care-plan` is passed. Schedules created by earlier imports are left as they are.

### When a send fails part-way

Every successful delivery is written to `client_reports.sent_to` straight away, before the next recipient is tried. If one address fails, the report stays `generated` (shown as **Partly sent** in the history table), the failure is logged, and the schedule stays due. The next run picks up the **same report** and only emails the addresses that are still missing. Clients who already got it never get a second copy, and you don't end up with a pile of duplicate reports while a mail outage lasts.

Manual runs:

```bash
php artisan clockwork:send-client-reports                   # only what's due
php artisan clockwork:send-client-reports --force           # ignore next_run_at
php artisan clockwork:send-client-reports --site=example.com  # one site (id or domain)
```

Careful: `--force` and **Send now** send for real on an `auto` schedule. A held report isn't emailed only because its fresh copy is checked again, and a schedule that has never sent always holds its report as the first; once a schedule has sent (`last_sent_at` set), a clean fresh copy goes out straight away. They also always compile **last month**, so from the 1st they no longer reach the previous month's drafts. To refresh a draft, use **Recompile**. On a dev copy, check `MAIL_MAILER=log` first.

## Refreshing held drafts (Recompile)

**Recompile** (`POST /client-reports/{report}/recompile`, route `client-reports.recompile`) refreshes an unsent scheduled report from current data. It's the safe way to pick up whatever changed since the report was compiled: hours logged, a scan rerun, a backup recorded, a fix in Control itself after a deploy.

- **Who gets the button:** scheduled reports that haven't reached anyone, i.e. status `generated` with an empty `sent_to` (`ClientReport::canRecompile()`). It's on the reports list, the client page and the operator preview, never on the public view; the reports list can also recompile every outdated report of a period, or the ticked ones, at once. Sent, partly sent, superseded and hand-generated reports are refused with a reason.
- **What it does:** `ScheduledReportRunner::recompile()` compiles the report's **own** `period_start`–`period_end` (not "last month"), with the schedule's current template and recipients, keeps any custom note, marks the old copy **superseded**, and runs the same review check as a scheduled run. An `auto` schedule that has never sent holds the new copy as its first report; a `draft` schedule doesn't add that reason. It runs in a transaction, so if compiling fails the old copy stays live.
- **What it never does:** send email, stamp `last_sent_at`, or move `next_run_at`. A recompiled copy with nothing to review still waits for someone to send it from the history table.
- **Outdated reports can't be sent as they are.** A report whose `meta.schema_version` is below `ClientReportCompiler::SCHEMA_VERSION` was compiled under older rules (`ClientReport::isStale()`). While nobody has it, the reports list shows **Outdated** with Send disabled, the operator preview says so, and `send()` refuses it ("Press Recompile …", or "Generate a new report …" for a hand-made one) without stamping `last_sent_at`. A scheduled run doesn't reuse such a copy either: it recompiles. A **Partly sent** or **Sent** report can still finish delivering or be resent, since its recipients already have that copy.

To refresh the drafts after a deploy:

1. Deploy and run the migrations, so the new data the compiler reads exists.
2. Run `php artisan clockwork:recompile-held-client-reports` (add `--dry-run` first to list them, `--outdated` for only those compiled under older rules, `--site=` for one site). It recompiles every unsent scheduled report the same way the button does and prints each new copy's review reasons; it never sends. Or open each held report (history → **Needs review** / **Outdated**) and press **Recompile**. You land on the new copy, with a message saying it was recompiled for its period and that nothing was sent, plus any reasons it's held for.
3. Check the new copy's review box and its **Email version**. Fix what you can and recompile again; send it by hand from the history table only once it's right.

Don't use **Send now** or `clockwork:send-client-reports --force` to refresh drafts: they compile last month only, and on a schedule that has sent before they email a clean report immediately.

## The email

`ClientReportMail` puts the whole report in the email body: an HTML view plus a plain-text one (`emails/report*.blade.php`). The logo is embedded inline via `BrandLogo` so it still shows when remote images are blocked. Update rows are cleaned up into client-friendly lines ("Example Plugin 3.3.6 → 3.3.7"). The greeting uses a first name only when the report goes to one address that is a known client contact whose stored name is plainly a person's; otherwise it says "Hi there,", in both parts. The From line is the configured sending address (`MAIL_FROM_ADDRESS`) under the report brand's company name, so the inbox shows the agency whatever `MAIL_FROM_NAME` says; the **Email version** page shows it. Reply-To is the report brand's support email. The footer text, when set, appears in both the HTML and plain-text parts.

There's deliberately **no "view online" link**: Control lives on a private network clients can't reach. That's also why the public `/reports/view/{token}` route exists but isn't linked anywhere useful.

## Branding

Colours, logo, company name and support details come from **Settings → Companion & White Label → Client Reports** (`/settings/companion?tab=reports`, stored as `reports.branding.*`). Blank fields fall back to the shared Companion identity. The support URL falls back from the reports value to the shared Companion support URL, then to the Agency Website; it never falls back to Control's own URL (`APP_URL`), which is private. The settings form only accepts public http(s) addresses there, not localhost or private-network hosts. The email doesn't link to it today; it's stored with each report. See [Companion & White Label](/docs/features/companion-branding).

The **primary colour** fills the header banner of both the email and the operator preview (and the section underlines); the logo sits on it, so use a logo that reads on that colour. The preview keeps the colours when printed or saved as a PDF. Because branding is captured in the snapshot, regenerate a report to see a colour change.
